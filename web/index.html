How the count is made

Learn the method

An atma beeja is a seed syllable kept with one’s own name and, when a chart is used, with a strong graha of that chart. It is not a general mantra picked from a list. The sixteen-house akathaha decides whether a letter may be worn by this person. A guru is the one who gives it. This page is the working method, with the passages it comes from.

What the beeja is

A letter, an akshara, is a sound. A beeja is a seed built from more than one sound, usually with a bindu. क is a letter. क्रीं is a seed. Several seeds make a mantra. The atma beeja is one seed placed with the person, often before and after another mantra. That enclosing is sampuṭa. The point of a personal seed is that each new mantra does not have to be tested from scratch against the name: the seed has already been chosen for this name and this chart.

The books

Two tantras give the chakra and the count.

Rudrayamala, patala 5. The chapter builds the maha-akathaha for the preparation of a mantra. Verses 2–3 say to draw a four-sided figure and divide it until there are sixteen houses. Verses 4–5 say to write the alphabet, from अ onward, through those houses in pradakshina order and then count. Verses 6–8 give the houses in code. The traditional unpacking of that code is the order 1, 3, 11, 9, 2, 4, 12, 10, 6, 8, 16, 14, 5, 7, 15, 13. Counting then runs from the first letter of the name to the first letter of the mantra. Verses 9–16 name the four steps siddha, sādhya, susiddha, and ari, and give the fruit of each pair.

Mantra Mahodadhi, taranga 24, of Mahidhara. Verses 1–2 say to purify a mantra from the first letter of the sādhaka’s name up to the first letter of the mantra, and that either the janma-nakshatra name or the name one is known by may be used. Verses 3–5 draw the same sixteen houses and fill them in the same order. Verses 6–12 explain the four groups of four. Verses 13–16 give the fruits. The commentary’s worked example puts द in house 3 and ऐ in house 14, and calls the meeting susiddha–ari. The figure in this app is that figure: its third house contains द, and its fourteenth contains ऐ.

The birth-chart refinement — start from the lagna lord’s bījākṣara when that lord is strong, otherwise from a pūrṇa yogakāraka, otherwise from a vowel or from क ब भ म र व श स ह, avoid the fourth, prefer the third, join रीं, and fall back to श्रीं — is a later working method taught on this same chakra. It is not a sūtra of Parashara. The app labels that layer as a working method, and it labels the chatushka layer as the tantra.

Varga deities follow the alphabet scheme of the Sharada Tilaka. Nakshatra deities follow Varahamihira, Brihat Samhita 98. Pada syllables follow the namakarana set of the Avakahada tradition, the set drik panchangas print. Vimshottari lords follow the usual order counted in Brihat Parashara Hora Shastra.

Letter and seed

Take only the base. In कुमार the first syllable is कु, which is क plus the vowel उ. The letter that enters the chakra is क. A pure vowel stays a vowel: Indira begins with इ.

The chart in use holds 49 letters. Sixteen houses, three letters each, would be 48; house 1 holds four, अ क थ ह, and the total is 49. ळ, क्ष, and ज्ञ have no house here. The Rudrayamala says the alphabet runs through क्ष. This working figure stops before those three. If a name begins with one of them, there is nothing to count, and the working method uses श्रीं.

Which name

Mantra Mahodadhi 24.2 allows two names. One is the name drawn from the janma nakshatra. The other is the name by which the person is actually known. Ashish Kumar, if everyone says Kumar, is entered as Kumar, not as Ashish. The app asks you to confirm the Sanskrit letter, because an English spelling does not decide between त and ट, or between श and ष.

Drawing the houses

Five lines down and five lines across make sixteen rooms. Number them left to right, top to bottom, 1 to 16. That numbering is the one in the commentary: house 3, house 14. Separately, the letters were appointed to those rooms in the fill order

1 · 3 · 11 · 9 · 2 · 4 · 12 · 10 · 6 · 8 · 16 · 14 · 5 · 7 · 15 · 13.

Read as four groups, the quartets are:

  1. Houses 1, 3, 11, 9
  2. Houses 2, 4, 12, 10
  3. Houses 6, 8, 16, 14
  4. Houses 5, 7, 15, 13

The quartet that contains the name becomes the siddha quartet. The next quartet in this cycle is sādhya, the next susiddha, the last ari. Inside the quartet where the mantra letter sits, the first house of that quartet, in the order written above, is siddha, the second sādhya, the third susiddha, the fourth ari. The two words together are the result: susiddha–ari, siddha–siddha, and the other fourteen.

Every letter in one house shares one result. You do not count from letter to letter inside the house. You count from house to house.

Chatushka count

Devadatta, testing a mantra that begins with ऐ.

The name begins with द, which is in house 3. House 3 is the second room of the first quartet (1, 3, 11, 9), so that whole quartet is siddha for him. ऐ is in house 14. House 14 is the last room of the third quartet (6, 8, 16, 14). The third quartet is susiddha. The last room of a quartet is ari. The commentary calls this susiddha–ari and says to leave it. The app reproduces that result.

Row and column

The atma-bija working method also counts in straight rows and columns, wrapping around the square as if it continued. The name’s own row is the first, called siddha. The next row down is sādhya. The third is susiddha. The fourth is ari. Columns move to the right in the same way. The rule used for choosing an atma beeja is short: the third row and the third column is the best square; the second and second is also spoken of as highly suitable; the fourth row or the fourth column is not used.

These four words are the same words as in the tantra, but they are not the same measurement. “Second row and second column” is not automatically the tantra’s “sādhya–sādhya”. The app shows both counts and does not collapse them into one.

Kumar, letter क, house 1.

र is in house 16. That is the fourth row and the fourth column. Both counts that the essay uses are ari, so a seed built on र is refused for this name.

म is in house 6. That is the second row and the second column. The essay treats the three letters of that house as suitable, then drops ऌ as inauspicious for an atma beeja, and keeps म rather than झ. It builds म्रीं. On the tantra’s own count, the same म is susiddha–siddha, which fructifies with half the japa. So the famous example is allowed by both counts. It is not, however, the third square.

The third square from house 1 is house 11, इ ग ध. Among those, ग is the opening of Guru’s seed, so the systematic beeja of the third square is ग्रीं. A published teacher chose the second square by hand. This app always shows the third square first, and keeps म्रीं in the suitable list, where that example can still be found.

A name that starts in the middle of the grid wraps. For स in house 13, two rows down and two columns across lands on house 7, औ ढ श. That is the same step Sanjay Rath’s note on this chakra uses when it calls the third square the best one.

Sixteen results

Mantra Mahodadhi 24.13–16, in the sense used here:

  • Siddha–siddha: in the time already set for that mantra.
  • Siddha–sādhya: twice the japa.
  • Siddha–susiddha: half the japa.
  • Siddha–ari: not used. The text says it harms kinsmen.
  • Sādhya–siddha: twice the japa.
  • Sādhya–sādhya: fruitless. Not used.
  • Sādhya–susiddha: twice the japa.
  • Sādhya–ari: not used. The text says it harms the gotra.
  • Susiddha–siddha: half the japa.
  • Susiddha–sādhya: twice the japa.
  • Susiddha–susiddha: from the initiation itself.
  • Susiddha–ari: not used. The text says it harms the family.
  • Ari–siddha: not used. The text says it harms sons.
  • Ari–sādhya: not used. The text says it harms daughters.
  • Ari–susiddha: not used. The text says it harms the spouse.
  • Ari–ari: not used. The text says it harms the practitioner.

The Rudrayamala’s wording of a few pairs is a little harsher or a little softer than Mahidhara’s. Where they differ, this app follows Mantra Mahodadhi, and it follows the atma-bija rule that a fourth row or fourth column is refused even if the other count sounds kind. The texts are telling a practitioner not to take those combinations. They are not instructions for harming anyone. An ari mantra already received is, in those books, to be set down with a guru’s rite. This app does not teach that rite. It only refuses the letter.

Making the seed

A bare letter is not yet a beeja. The working method joins the chosen consonant to रीं. The reason given there is that र belongs to Agni and ईं is kāmakalā. So म becomes म्रीं, ग becomes ग्रीं, श becomes श्रीं. When the letter under test is र itself, the seed is रीं and is not written twice.

A vowel can take a bindu — इ becomes इं — but the essays want a real seed, not only a letter with a dot. If the winning house contains a consonant, the consonant is used. ऌ is never used, even in a good house. The published note on ऌ is simply that it is not auspicious for this purpose.

If every acceptable house fails, the same essays say to use श्रीं. They call it a fruition seed. It is also the ordinary joining of श with रीं, so when श itself wins a good house the beeja is श्रीं for a structural reason, not only as a fallback.

The birth chart

The working method says the letter can be taken from the horoscope, then still has to pass the chakra.

  1. If the lagna lord is strong — the essays mention high shadbala, a good house, no combustion, no debilitation — use that graha’s bījākṣara, provided the chakra does not refuse it.
  2. If the lagna lord fails, and the lagna is one that has a single yogakaraka, use that graha the same way. The six lagnas are Taurus (Shani), Cancer (Mangala), Leo (Mangala), Libra (Shani), Capricorn (Shukra), and Aquarius (Shukra). Each of those grahas owns both a kendra and a trikona.
  3. Otherwise choose from the good letters in the third square, then from the other houses the chakra still allows.

The nine graha seeds used here are the usual navagraha bījas:

  • Surya ह्रां — ॐ ह्रां ह्रीं ह्रौं सः सूर्याय नमः
  • Chandra श्रां — ॐ श्रां श्रीं श्रौं सः चन्द्राय नमः
  • Mangala क्रां — ॐ क्रां क्रीं क्रौं सः भौमाय नमः
  • Budha ब्रां — ॐ ब्रां ब्रीं ब्रौं सः बुधाय नमः
  • Guru ग्रां — ॐ ग्रां ग्रीं ग्रौं सः गुरवे नमः
  • Shukra द्रां — ॐ द्रां द्रीं द्रौं सः शुक्राय नमः
  • Shani प्रां — ॐ प्रां प्रीं प्रौं सः शनैश्चराय नमः
  • Rahu भ्रां — ॐ भ्रां भ्रीं भ्रौं सः राहवे नमः
  • Ketu स्रां — ॐ स्रां स्रीं स्रौं सः केतवे नमः

When the chart’s graha survives the chakra, the primary seed is that graha’s own beeja, and the joining with रीं is shown beside it. When the graha’s letter falls in ari, it is dropped even if the planet is strong. That is the Kumar case, moved from a hand-chosen र onto the chart: a strong Mars still cannot donate क if क sits on the fourth.

The strength screen in this app is narrower than shadbala. A graha passes when it is not debilitated, not combust, and stands in a kendra (1, 4, 7, 10), a trikona (1, 5, 9), its own sign, moolatrikona, or exaltation. Combustion orbs used are Moon 12°, Mars 17°, Mercury 14° (12° if retrograde), Jupiter 11°, Venus 10° (8° if retrograde), Saturn 15°. Neecha-bhanga is not applied. A natural malefic is not failed merely for being a malefic, because Shani is the yogakaraka of Taurus and Libra. If you need shadbala, this screen is not it.

Janma letter

Each nakshatra is 13°20′, and each of its four padas is 3°20′. The Moon’s pada at birth supplies a syllable. The base of that syllable is a lawful reference letter under Mantra Mahodadhi 24.2. Ashvini 1 is चू, so the base is च. Krittika 1 is the vowel अ. Pushya 4 is डा, retroflex ड, not dental द.

Shravana is taken here as खी खू खे खो, which is the set printed with drik panchangas. Some books use जु जे जो घ. If your family panchanga uses the other set, choose that letter by hand.

Without a birth time the pada can be wrong, because the Moon moves about one nakshatra a day. The app will still compute a noon chart if you say the time is unknown, and it will not let that lagna choose the beeja.

Deities

The Sharada scheme used here, as set out in the alphabet tradition of that tantra:

  • All vowels: graha Surya, devata Sadashiva.
  • Ka-varga क ख ग घ ङ: Mangala and Brahma. Ca-varga च छ ज झ ञ: Shukra and Brahma.
  • Retroflex ta ट ठ ड ढ ण: Budha and Vishnu. Dental ta त थ द ध न: Guru and Vishnu.
  • Pa-varga प फ ब भ म: Shani and Shiva. Ya-varga, taken here as य र व श: Chandra and Shiva.
  • Sha group ष स ह ल: Chandra and Rudra. One tabulation places visarga with the lunar semivowels; this app keeps अः with the vowels and says so on that letter.

Several letters also carry a shakti-name from that alphabet gloss: क Srishti, ख Vriddhi, ग Smriti, घ Medha, ङ Kanti, च Lakshmi, छ Dhriti, ज Sthira, झ Sthiti, ञ Siddhi, and so on through the vowels. Pa, pha, ba, bha, ma, ya, ra, and va are not given an extra shakti-name here, because the gloss list that supplied the others does not state one cleanly. Inventing one would be worse than leaving the varga deity.

Worship follows the letter you actually received. If the chart gives Mangala and क survives, the worship is Mangala’s seed and mantra, and Brahma remains the varga devata of क. If the chakra alone gives म्रीं, the varga devata is Shiva and the varga graha is Shani. If the reference letter was the janma syllable, the nakshatra deity is named as well. None of this replaces the devata of a mantra your guru has already given.

Ayanamsha

The chart is sidereal. The ayanamsha is JN Bhasin, Swiss Ephemeris mode SE_SIDM_JN_BHASIN (number 8). Lahiri is calculated beside it so you can see the gap, which is a little over a degree through the twentieth and twenty-first centuries. The houses and the nakshatra are drawn from JN Bhasin only. Drik Panchang’s own public charts are Lahiri. “Drik” here means the astronomical ephemeris, not a copy of that website and not Lahiri.

Positions use the Swiss Ephemeris Moshier theory, which travels inside the library and needs no extra ephemeris files. It is more than tight enough to decide a sign and a pada. Between the years 1800 and 2200 it is at its best. Outside that, the app warns you. Rahu defaults to the mean node. Ketu is the opposite point. Whole-sign houses: the sign of the ascendant is house 1 in full, the next sign is house 2, and so on.

Reading the chart

The South Indian figure keeps the signs still. Pisces, Aries, Taurus, and Gemini stand on the top row; Aquarius and Cancer on the next; Capricorn and Leo under them; Sagittarius, Scorpio, Libra, and Virgo along the bottom. Planets move into the sign they occupy. The lagna sign is marked. The house number is how far that sign is from the lagna, counting the lagna as 1.

The North Indian figure keeps the houses still. House 1 is the top diamond, and the signs move. Both figures are the same chart. A retrograde planet is marked in copper and with R. The table under the figures gives degree, nakshatra, pada, dignity, and combustion.

The opening Vimshottari mahadasha is the lord of the Moon’s nakshatra. The balance is the unwalked part of that nakshatra, times the lord’s years. It is the balance at birth, not a full 120-year table.

Using it

The essays say the guru gives the seed, and the student repeats it for a few days as a trial. A quiet, bright mind is taken as a sign that the human count was not mistaken. Disturbance is a sign to stop and to choose the next lawful seed, or श्रीं. Once it sits well, it is placed before a mantra, or before and after it. Advanced practice is sometimes the seed alone, laid on the breath. Those are instructions for someone already inside a practice. They are not a claim that a website has initiated you.

The shape of sampuṭa is: seed, then the mantra, then the seed. The app shows that shape with the words “your mantra” left blank on purpose.

Limits

  • It does not give diksha, and it does not replace a guru.
  • It does not compute shadbala, ashtakavarga, or neecha-bhanga.
  • It uses one akathaha and two published ways of reading it. Other paddhatis exist. The books themselves say there is more than one method.
  • English-to-Sanskrit is a hint. The letter you tap is the letter that is counted.
  • The harmful fruits in the ari lines are reported because they are in Mantra Mahodadhi. They are reasons to leave the letter, not a practice.
